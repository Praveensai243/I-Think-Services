# International — GDPR, UK, Canada

Applies when the people are there, regardless of where the business is. A US company with EU
users, EU staff, or EU contractors is in scope.

**Verify before asserting.** Primary sources: the GDPR text (Regulation 2016/679), the UK ICO
guidance, and CASL/PIPEDA guidance from Canada.

## The lawful basis comes first

Under GDPR you cannot process personal data without a lawful basis, chosen before processing:

- **Consent** — freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, by clear affirmative action.
  Withdrawable as easily as it was given. Cannot be bundled or pre-ticked. Not valid where
  there is a power imbalance (employees).
- **Contract** — necessary to perform a contract with that person.
- **Legal obligation**, **vital interests**, **public task**.
- **Legitimate interests** — the flexible one, requiring a documented balancing test and an
  absolute right to object for direct marketing.

**Consent is not automatically the safest choice.** It is the most fragile: withdrawable at
any moment, and if you rely on it you must stop when it is withdrawn. For B2B email marketing,
legitimate interests plus a clean opt-out is often more durable — but it requires the balancing
assessment written down.

## Where GDPR is stricter than US practice in ways that break designs

- **No pre-ticked boxes. No implied consent. Silence is not consent.**
- **Withdrawal must be as easy as giving** — a one-click opt-in and a phone-call-only opt-out
  is a violation.
- **Cookie/tracker consent must come before setting non-essential cookies**, and "reject" must
  be as prominent as "accept". A banner that only offers "Accept" fails; so does one where
  rejecting takes three more clicks.
- **Breach notification within 72 hours** to the supervisory authority.
- **Data protection by design and by default** — the privacy-protective setting is the one
  that ships.
- **DPIA** required for high-risk processing, including large-scale profiling and some AI uses.
- **Automated decision-making** with legal or significant effects gets special protection.
- **Records of processing** must be maintained.

## Fines

Up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher, for the more
serious categories. UK GDPR mirrors this in sterling.

## International transfers

Moving EU/UK personal data to the US needs a valid mechanism — Standard Contractual Clauses,
an adequacy decision, or a certification framework. Check the **current** status: adequacy
arrangements between the EU and US have been invalidated twice by the Court of Justice, and
anything you recall about the present framework may be out of date. Verify before advising.

## Canada

- **CASL** — consent-based commercial electronic messages, express or implied, with implied
  consent time-limited. Identification and unsubscribe requirements. Penalties are severe and
  it applies to anyone messaging into Canada.
- **PIPEDA** — federal privacy law; some provinces have their own substantially similar laws.

## Audit checklist

- [ ] Lawful basis identified and documented per processing purpose
- [ ] Where consent is the basis: unbundled, unticked, withdrawable as easily as given
- [ ] Legitimate-interests balancing test written down where relied on
- [ ] Cookie banner blocks non-essential cookies until consent; reject as prominent as accept
- [ ] Privacy notice covers basis, retention, rights, transfers, and the controller's identity
- [ ] Transfer mechanism in place and currently valid
- [ ] DPIA where processing is high-risk
- [ ] 72-hour breach process defined before it is needed
- [ ] Records of processing maintained
